Day Privacy Policy

Day is a Mac app that puts an event on your existing calendar in one line, developed by Pavel Zarudnev. In this policy, “I” means Pavel Zarudnev. This page explains what Day does with your information, and what happens when you contact support or visit these pages.

Effective date: 24 November 2026

At a glance

  • Day has no account, no Day-operated server, no analytics, no advertising, and no tracking SDKs.
  • Type one line, check the preview, press Return. Day writes the event to your existing calendar through macOS Calendar, after you grant access.
  • Calendar access is requested when you save your first event, or from the banner or Settings. Typing and previewing work without it.
  • Notes and tasks are optional Markdown files in a folder you choose. Day has no sync service of its own.
  • Day and its widgets share local preferences and a local snapshot through an app group on your Mac.
  • If you choose a synced folder or a calendar account, the provider you selected handles that sync under its own policy.
  • Support email is voluntary; this website's host may process standard request data.

Notes and tasks stay in your folder

Notes are optional and off by default. If you choose a folder, Day stores one Markdown file per day, named YYYY-MM-DD.md. Day reads and writes those files when you add, edit, complete, defer, schedule, or delete a line. A security-scoped bookmark is stored locally so Day can reopen the folder after a relaunch.

The Markdown files are your record. Day does not upload them to a Day-operated server. You can edit or delete them in the folder you selected; if that folder is in a synced location, the provider may keep copies or backups under its own policy.

Day has no account, sign-in, hosted sync, analytics, advertising, or tracking SDK. Its own code contains no network client.

Calendar access is optional

Day asks for Calendar access when you press Return to save your first event, or when you choose to grant it from the banner or Settings. Until then you can type and preview; nothing is written to your calendar.

If you grant access, Day uses Apple's EventKit to display events from macOS Calendar and to create, edit, or cancel events when you ask. Calendar data stays with macOS and your calendar provider, except for the local information Day uses for planning and widgets. Day does not send it to Pavel Zarudnev or a Day-operated server, and Day never uploads it.

If you deny or later revoke access, scheduling cannot save, but Day keeps your text and explains what to change. Notes and tasks keep working. You can change access in System Settings → Privacy & Security → Calendars.

Calendar accounts such as iCloud, Google, or Exchange are managed by macOS and your provider. Their sync and privacy practices are their own.

Widgets and local cache

Day's widget extension reads a local snapshot from the app group shared by the app and the widget. The snapshot contains event titles, times, and identifiers; open task text and line references; and meeting note counts. Pending task toggles are queued as local files in the same app group container. Day uses this data to draw the widget, to open an event in Day when you click it, and to apply a task toggle when the app next runs.

Checking off a task or adding a meeting note from a widget needs a notes folder. Without one, the widget shows calendar events only.

This widget data stays on your Mac. Day does not send it to a Day-operated server. The app and widget also share local preferences, such as your time format, through the same app group.

Sync you choose

Day does not sync your files and has no sync service of its own. If you put the notes folder in iCloud Drive, Dropbox, OneDrive, or another synced location, that provider uploads and stores the Markdown files under its own terms and privacy policy. Day does not control or see that sync. The same applies to calendar accounts you add to macOS.

External meeting links

Day can find a meeting link in an event's URL, location, or notes and show a Join button. Day does not open the link until you click it. The link then opens in your default browser or the app macOS chooses. The browser and the meeting service may receive standard request information, such as your IP address and the link, under their own policies. Day does not send the link to Pavel Zarudnev.

Support email you send voluntarily

If you email pzarudnev93@gmail.com, you choose what to include. I receive your email address, your message, and any attachments you send. I use this information to answer your request and troubleshoot the issue.

Support correspondence may be retained to respond to your request, follow up, or meet legal obligations. You can email me to request deletion of correspondence you’ve sent. The email provider also processes the messages under its own policies.

Please don’t send private notes, calendar exports, or unredacted screenshots. The support page explains how to report an issue without private attachments. If you are testing a TestFlight build, you can also send feedback from the TestFlight app.

This website

These pages do not use analytics or advertising scripts. The hosting and delivery providers may process standard request data, such as your IP address, browser information, requested URL, and time, to serve and protect the site. Those providers handle service logs under their own policies.

Changes to this policy

If this policy changes, I will update the effective date and the text on this page. The current version is always at this URL.
